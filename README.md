# Telecom Customer Churn Analysis

### Identifying Churn Drivers, High-Risk Customers and Retention Opportunities

Customer churn is one of the most expensive problems in subscription-based businesses because every customer lost reduces recurring revenue and increases the pressure to replace that customer through acquisition.

This project analysed **7,043 telecom customers across 21 customer, service and billing variables** to understand:

* why customers were leaving;
* which customers were most at risk;
* which business factors were associated with churn;
* and where management could intervene to improve retention.

The analysis identified an overall churn rate of **26.5%**, representing **1,869 customers who left the company**.

The strongest churn patterns were linked to **contract type, monthly charges, payment method, customer tenure and support-service adoption**, providing clear areas for customer-retention action.

# Business Problem

The company was losing more than one in every four customers.

The key business questions were:

* Which customers are most likely to churn?
* What factors are associated with customer attrition?
* Are there controllable business factors that management can address?
* Which customer groups should receive retention attention first?
* What interventions could reduce future churn?

Rather than treating churn as a single percentage, the analysis broke the problem into customer, contract, billing and service factors to identify where the strongest risk patterns existed.

# Key Findings

## 1. Month-to-Month Contracts Had the Highest Churn

Contract type was one of the clearest churn signals in the analysis.

Customers on **month-to-month contracts recorded the highest churn**, while customers on longer-term contracts were more likely to remain with the company.

### Business Interpretation

Month-to-month contracts give customers greater flexibility to leave.

That flexibility becomes particularly important when customers are dissatisfied with pricing, service quality or perceived value.

This makes contract structure one of the most actionable retention areas available to management.

### Opportunity

Instead of treating all customers equally, the company could identify profitable month-to-month customers and encourage them to move to longer-term contracts through suitable retention offers.

---

# 2. Higher Monthly Charges Were Associated With Higher Churn

Customers with **higher monthly charges showed stronger churn patterns**, particularly among month-to-month customers.

### Business Interpretation

The relationship suggests that customers may become more sensitive to value when recurring charges are high.

A high monthly bill is not automatically a problem if customers believe the service provides sufficient value.

However, high prices combined with weak contract commitment may create a stronger churn risk.

### Opportunity

The business should examine:

* customers paying high monthly charges;
* services included in those plans;
* customer tenure;
* support usage;
* contract type.

This would help distinguish high-value customers from customers who may simply feel that they are paying too much.

---

# 3. Electronic Check Customers Had the Highest Churn

Customers using **Electronic Check** recorded the highest churn among the payment methods analysed.

### Business Interpretation

Payment method itself may not directly cause churn.

However, it can act as an indicator of customer behaviour or commitment.

Automatic payment methods may reduce friction in the billing process and may also be more common among customers with stronger long-term relationships.

### Opportunity

Customers currently using Electronic Check could be encouraged to move toward automatic payment options where appropriate.

This should be tested as part of a broader retention strategy rather than treated as a standalone solution.

---

# 4. Customers Without Technical Support Churned More Frequently

Customers without **Technical Support and related value-added services** showed higher churn patterns.

### Business Interpretation

This suggests that customer-support services may contribute to perceived value and loyalty.

Customers who encounter problems but do not have access to support may be more likely to leave instead of resolving the issue.

### Opportunity

Rather than only promoting Technical Support as an additional service, management could investigate whether support adoption reduces customer frustration and improves retention among specific customer groups.

---

# 5. New and Early-Tenure Customers Were the Most Vulnerable

Customers earlier in their relationship with the company were more likely to churn than longer-tenured customers.

### Business Interpretation

The beginning of the customer relationship appears to be a particularly important retention period.

If customers do not quickly experience enough value from the service, they may leave before developing a stronger relationship with the company.

### Opportunity

The company should treat the first stage of the customer lifecycle as a high-risk period.

Potential interventions include:

* stronger onboarding;
* early satisfaction checks;
* product education;
* proactive service support;
* targeted retention offers.

---

# High-Risk Customer Profile

When the strongest churn indicators are combined, the highest-risk customer profile in the analysis is:

> **New customer + Month-to-month contract + High monthly charges + Electronic Check + No Technical Support**

This is one of the most useful outputs of the project because it gives the business a clearer customer segment to monitor.

Instead of waiting until customers leave, the company could proactively identify customers matching several of these conditions and intervene earlier.

---

# Main Business Insight

The strongest churn patterns were connected more closely to **operational and commercial factors** than to basic customer demographics.

The most important factors were:

* Contract type
* Monthly charges
* Payment method
* Customer tenure
* Technical Support and service adoption

This matters because several of these factors are at least partially controllable by the business.

That means the organisation has opportunities to reduce churn through changes to customer experience, pricing, retention programmes and service design.

---

# Recommendations

## 1. Prioritise Month-to-Month Customers

Month-to-month customers should be treated as a priority retention group.

Management can test incentives that encourage suitable customers to move toward longer-term agreements.

Possible approaches include:

* discounted contract upgrades;
* loyalty benefits;
* bundled services;
* renewal incentives.

The objective should not be to force long-term contracts, but to give valuable customers stronger reasons to stay.

---

## 2. Review High-Charge Customer Plans

Customers with high monthly charges should be assessed for:

* service usage;
* customer tenure;
* contract type;
* support-service adoption;
* overall value received.

Where customers are paying high prices but receiving limited perceived value, pricing or package redesign may be necessary.

---

## 3. Strengthen Early Customer Onboarding

Because new customers are especially vulnerable, the first year should receive greater retention attention.

The company should consider:

* onboarding communication;
* early satisfaction surveys;
* proactive support;
* education on available services;
* first-year loyalty incentives.

The objective is to establish value before customers reach the point of considering cancellation.

---

## 4. Promote Support and Value-Added Services

Customers without Technical Support churned more often.

Support-service adoption should therefore be investigated as part of the retention strategy.

The company could test whether making Technical Support, Online Security or Device Protection more accessible improves customer retention in high-risk segments.

---

## 5. Encourage Lower-Friction Payment Methods

Electronic Check customers showed the highest churn among payment methods.

Management could encourage customers to use automatic payment options where appropriate.

The effectiveness of this intervention should be monitored rather than assumed.

---

## 6. Build a Proactive Churn-Monitoring System

Instead of only reporting churn after customers leave, the business could create an early-warning process based on the risk profile identified in the analysis.

Customers could be flagged when they exhibit several high-risk characteristics, such as:

* short tenure;
* month-to-month contract;
* high monthly charges;
* Electronic Check;
* no Technical Support.

Retention teams could then prioritise outreach before cancellation occurs.

---

# Potential Business Impact

This analysis does not calculate the exact financial value of reducing churn because the available project data does not establish the full lifetime value or acquisition cost of each customer.

However, reducing churn would reasonably support:

* stronger recurring revenue;
* higher customer lifetime value;
* lower replacement/acquisition pressure;
* improved customer loyalty;
* more predictable revenue.

The largest opportunity is not simply identifying customers who have already churned.

It is using the identified patterns to **recognise risk before the customer leaves**.

# Analytical Limitations

The project identifies patterns associated with customer churn.

It does **not** establish that these variables directly caused customers to leave.

For example:

> Electronic Check customers had higher churn.

That does not mean Electronic Check itself causes churn.

Similarly:

> Customers without Technical Support churned more frequently.

This does not prove that adding Technical Support alone would prevent churn.

Other factors may influence these relationships.

The findings should therefore be used to prioritise further investigation and business experimentation.

# What This Project Shows

The most important outcome of this project was not simply calculating a **26.5% churn rate**.

It was identifying where that churn was concentrated.

The analysis showed that the customers most at risk shared several characteristics:

> shorter tenure, month-to-month contracts, higher monthly charges, Electronic Check payments and lower support-service adoption.

That changes the business question from:

> **“How many customers are leaving?”**

to:

> **“Which customers are most likely to leave, and what can we do before they do?”**

That is the point where reporting becomes useful business analysis.
